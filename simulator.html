    How it works

    1. Every trade is a dice roll. With a 50% win rate, each trade has a 50% chance of winning your risk times your R, and otherwise loses your risk. Fees come off every trade.
    2. Every evaluation is a run of trades. After each trade it checks the rules like a referee: hit the target and you pass, touch the drawdown or the daily limit and you fail, run out of days and it's unfinished.
    3. Then it repeats 10,000 times. One attempt tells you nothing, the same way one coin flip can't tell you if a coin is fair. Thousands of attempts show you the real odds.

    The drawdown types

    • End-of-day trailing: the fail line moves up to sit "max drawdown" below your best closing balance, checked once a day.
    • Intraday trailing: the fail line moves up after every trade. Stricter, because a winning morning followed by a losing afternoon tightens it straight away. Real firms track open profit too, so the real thing is stricter still.
    • Static: the fail line never moves. The most forgiving.

    A trailing drawdown is like a ratchet: it only ever moves up. Most firms stop it once it reaches your starting balance.

    What it doesn't know

    • It treats every trade as independent. Real traders tilt after losses, and markets have streaky weeks. Read the result as a best case.
    • Consistency rules (like "your best day can't be more than half your profit") and minimum trading days aren't included.
    • Firms change their rules often. The presets are a starting point, so check your firm's current rules and type them in.
    • Your win rate is a guess until you have a lot of trades. 10 trades can't tell you your real win rate; 100 is a start.

    This is an education tool, not a prediction or financial advice. Most people fail prop firm evaluations, so only pay for one you can afford to lose.
