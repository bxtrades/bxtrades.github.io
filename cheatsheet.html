01Candlesticks

Each candle is one chunk of time. The body runs from open to close. The wicks show the high and the low. Green means it closed higher than it opened, red means lower.

More →

02Timeframes

Use a higher timeframe (1h, 4h) for direction and a lower one (1m, 5m) to time the entry. Like using a map to choose the road, then the satnav for each turn.

More →

03Trends and ranges

  • Uptrend: higher highs and higher lows
  • Downtrend: lower highs and lower lows
  • Range: price bouncing sideways between two levels
More →

04Support and resistance

Support is a floor where buyers have stepped in before. Resistance is a ceiling where sellers have. When a level breaks, it often flips: an old ceiling becomes the new floor.

More →

05Liquidity and sweeps

Stop losses pile up just above highs and just below lows, especially equal highs and lows. A sweep is when price pokes through, takes those stops, and then turns around.

More →

06Break of structure (BOS)

Price closes past the last swing high (bullish) or swing low (bearish). It tells you the move has real strength, not just a wick poking through.

More →

07Fair value gap (FVG)

A three-candle pattern where the wicks of candle 1 and candle 3 don't overlap. The gap shows price moved too fast, and it often comes back to fill part of it.

More →

08Sessions (UK time)

  • Asia: overnight, usually quiet
  • London: opens 8am
  • New York: stocks open 9:30am NY time, usually 2:30pm UK (an hour earlier for a few weeks in March and around late October or early November, when the clocks change on different dates)

10Risk and R

1R is what you risk on a trade. A target twice as far as your stop is 2R. With 2R trades you can be wrong more often than right and still come out ahead: win 4 out of 10 and you're up 2R (8R won, 6R lost).

More →

11Before every eval

  • Know your max drawdown, and whether it trails
  • Know the daily loss limit, if there is one
  • Size every trade from your stop, not from a feeling
  • Decide how many losses ends your day, before you start
  • Practise on paper until it's boring
