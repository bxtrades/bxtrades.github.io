The basics

Candlestick
One bar on a chart showing four prices for a period of time: where it opened, where it closed, and the highest and lowest points (the wicks). Green or hollow means it closed higher than it opened; red or filled means lower. ▶ Watch my video
Timeframe
How much time each candle covers: 1 minute, 5 minutes, 1 hour, 1 day. Higher timeframes show the big picture; lower timeframes show the detail used to time an entry. ▶ Watch my video
Trend vs range
A trend is price making higher highs and higher lows (up) or lower highs and lower lows (down). A range is price bouncing sideways between a ceiling and a floor. ▶ Watch my video
Support and resistance
Price levels where the market has reacted before. Support is a floor where buyers stepped in; resistance is a ceiling where sellers did. ▶ Watch my video
Trading sessions
The main parts of the trading day: Asia, London and New York. The London high or Asia low just means the highest or lowest price reached during that session. ▶ Watch my video
Paper trading
Trading a simulated account with fake money but real prices. It's how you practise a strategy without risking anything.

Price action ideas

Liquidity
Clusters of waiting orders, mostly stop losses, that sit just above obvious highs and just below obvious lows. Big players need those orders to fill their own trades. ▶ Watch my video
Liquidity sweep
When price pokes just past a high or low, triggers the stop losses sitting there, then quickly reverses. Also called a stop hunt or sweeping out liquidity.
Draw on liquidity
The level price looks likely to head for next, usually an untouched high or low with lots of stops resting beyond it.
Break of structure (BOS)
When price breaks past the last significant high (in an uptrend) or low (in a downtrend), suggesting the trend is continuing. ▶ Watch my video
Fair value gap (FVG)
A three-candle pattern from a fast move, where the wicks of the first and third candles don't overlap. The gap left behind is an area price often comes back to. ▶ Watch my video
Inversion FVG
A fair value gap that price closes straight through. It then flips its role: a bearish gap that gets closed above can act as support, and a bullish gap closed below can act as resistance. In my notes this is inversed.
Retest
When price breaks a level, then comes back to touch it before carrying on. Many traders wait for the retest, and a rejection from it, before entering.
SMT divergence
When two markets that normally move together, like ES and NQ, disagree at a key point. For example, NQ makes a lower low but ES doesn't. It can be a hint that the move is running out of steam.
AMD
Accumulation, manipulation, distribution: a way of describing a session. Price builds a range (accumulation), fakes a move one way, often sweeping liquidity (manipulation), then makes the real move the other way (distribution).

Risk and prop firms

R and risk-to-reward (RR)
1R is the amount you risk on a trade. Making 2.29R means you made 2.29 times what you risked. Risk-to-reward compares how far your target is to how far your stop is.
Prop firm and evaluation
A proprietary trading firm lets you trade its money if you pass an evaluation: hit a profit target without breaking its rules. You pay a fee for each attempt.
Max drawdown
The most your account is allowed to lose before the evaluation fails. A trailing drawdown moves up as your account makes new highs, so it can catch you out.
ES, NQ, MES and MNQ
Futures on the S&P 500 (ES) and Nasdaq-100 (NQ). NQ is $20 per point per contract, ES $50. The micros, MNQ and MES, are a tenth of that: $2 and $5.
